The free system-wide parametric equalizer for Mac

A free, open-source EQ for every sound your Mac plays: music, browsers, games and calls. No audio driver, no kernel extension, no account.

Download for macOS

Version 0.6.0 · macOS 14.2 or later · universal build, developed on Apple silicon · Source on GitHub

EQ is a Mac equalizer that applies a parametric EQ to all system audio, on speakers or headphones. Shape the sound by dragging points on a frequency-response curve, the way you would in a studio EQ plugin, and hear the change immediately.

Unlike most Mac equalizers it does not install a virtual audio device. It uses the Core Audio process tap API Apple added in macOS 14.2, so there is nothing to uninstall and your output device and volume keys keep working as usual. See how it works.

Features

System-wide

One EQ for everything the Mac plays, with no per-app setup.

Fully parametric

Up to 16 bands, each with its own frequency (20 Hz to 20 kHz), gain (±24 dB) and Q.

Five filter types

Peak, low shelf, high shelf, low pass and high pass.

Graphic mode too

5, 10, 15 or 31 sliders at the standard frequencies, joined by a smooth curve.

Frequency sweep

Play a sine sweep through the EQ, press Space where your speakers or headphones ring, and turn the mark into a cut.

Volume-matched A/B

Compare before and after a change at the same loudness, so louder never fools your ears.

Bass, treble and balance

Tone shelves at 100 Hz and 10 kHz and a left/right balance, in both modes.

Presets

Flat, Bass, Treble and Vocal built in, and your own saved by name. Applying one can be undone.

AutoEq headphone presets

Import a headphone correction from AutoEq, or any Equalizer APO ParametricEQ.txt file.

A curve for each output

Speakers and headphones each remember their own EQ and get it back when you switch.

Clip protection

A built-in limiter keeps heavy boosts from distorting. On by default.

Lives in the menu bar

Close the window and the EQ keeps running. Switch presets or bypass from the menu bar, and open at login.

Private

Audio is processed on your Mac and never leaves it. The app makes no network connections.

Native feel

Light and dark appearance, your accent color, undo and redo, and a full menu bar.

Install

  1. Download EQ.zip from the latest release and unzip it.
  2. Move EQ.app to Applications and open it.
  3. The app is not notarized by Apple, so macOS blocks the first launch. Open System Settings › Privacy & Security, scroll down, and click Open Anyway.
  4. Allow system audio capture when macOS asks. If you hear nothing, enable EQ under System Settings › Privacy & Security › Screen & System Audio Recording.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need BlackHole, Soundflower or another virtual audio driver?

No. EQ captures system audio with a Core Audio process tap, which is built into macOS 14.2 and later.

Is EQ free?

Yes. EQ is free and open source under the GPL-3.0 license, with no account and no in-app purchases.

Which Macs does it run on?

Any Mac on macOS 14.2 (Sonoma) or later. The download is a universal build; it is developed on Apple silicon and the Intel half has not been tested yet.

How do I use an AutoEq headphone profile on a Mac?

Find your headphones on autoeq.app, choose “EqualizerAPO ParametricEq” and download the file. Import it with Presets › Import…, or drop it on the window. Read the full guide.

Why does macOS ask for permission to record audio?

Reading system audio is what lets the app equalize it, and macOS guards that behind the “System Audio Recording” permission. Nothing is recorded or saved, and the app makes no network connections.

How do I uninstall it?

Turn off EQ › Open at Login if you enabled it, quit the app and delete EQ.app. No drivers or background services are installed.
